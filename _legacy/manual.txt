Osman Interlinings Limited (OIL)
Comprehensive Procurement Process &amp; Automation Specification
Document ID: OIL-PMS-BRD-001 Version: 1.0 — Proposed / For Review Date: 11 October 2026 Process owner: Procurement Department Stakeholders: Marketing, Store, Quality Assurance (QA), Procurement, Finance &amp; Accounts, Import/Logistics, Executive Director (ED) / Delegated Final Approver, Suppliers, IT System name (proposed): OIL Procurement Management System (OIL-PMS)
Purpose. Define an end-to-end, auditable, software-ready workflow for procuring interlining raw materials at OIL. This is a proposed target-state process, not an assertion that every policy or approval limit has already been adopted. Policy choices requiring confirmation are identified in Section 17.
1. Executive summary
OIL-PMS will centralize purchase requisitions, consolidate compatible requirements, solicit supplier quotes using expiring secure links, collect structured commercial/technical offers, record sample-testing results, compare and negotiate bids, obtain Marketing acknowledgment and Finance/ED approvals, and automatically generate and email a Purchase Order (PO) following final approval. The process continues through supplier acknowledgment, Proforma Invoice (PI) validation, import execution, goods receipt, invoice matching, and payment closure.
Target outcomes: (1) process discipline and traceability, (2) informed, quality-compliant supplier selection, (3) competitive landed-cost procurement, (4) faster cycle time, and (5) real-time management visibility.
Key design rules:
Every request and transaction has a unique identifier and responsible owner.
Every invited supplier receives an individual, restricted RFQ URL—not a shared open quotation page.
RFQ deadlines use the supplier-country calendar, initially China (Asia/Shanghai, UTC+08:00) , including official holidays and adjusted make-up working days.
Supplier quotes remain confidential from competing suppliers; commercial information is released internally by role and evaluation stage.
Sample/technical compliance is independently recorded before award; an authorized waiver is documented where testing is not required.
Marketing ACK is an acknowledgment, Finance APP is financial approval, and ED/delegated authority provides final approval .
PO email is automatic only after final approval, PO completeness checks, and a valid supplier email address.
PO/PI variations must be detected, documented and, when material, reapproved.
2. Scope and boundaries
In scope
Domestic or overseas purchases of interlining-related raw materials, with overseas/import sourcing as the primary reference flow.
Requirements raised by Marketing or Store; consolidation and procurement validation.
Supplier shortlist, RFQ, supplier data entry, secure submissions and reminders.
Technical/quality evaluation and sampling, supplier comparison, negotiation and approval.
Auto-PO generation, supplier acknowledgment, PI checking, shipment, GRN, supplier invoicing and settlement.
Reporting, document repository, notifications, role-based access and audit evidence.
Separate or conditional branches
Domestic purchase: may bypass LC/TT and customs steps while retaining PO, receipt, QA, three-way match and payment.
Repeat/approved material: technical testing may be waived under an approved, logged rule.
Single-source/emergency purchase: requires documented justification and approval; cannot silently bypass governance.
Services or capital purchases: may require separate field templates and approval policies.

Common status rules
Return sends the file to a named prior owner with a mandatory reason; returned items never appear as approved.

4. Detailed process instructions and validation gates
01 — Requisition creation
Actions: Marketing or Store selects RM item/specification (or proposes a new item), requested quantity/UOM, desired delivery date, delivery location, business justification, intended use, urgency and attachments. Checks: mandatory-field completeness; user authorization; active item master; duplication warnings; available stock and pending PO visibility. Output: PR-YYYY-NNNNN ; status Submitted . Exception: missing specifications return to originator; urgency flag does not bypass approval.
02 — Consolidation
Actions: Procurement views open PRs together and groups compatible demand by item, technical specification, shipment window and delivery location; optionally splits a PR across RFQs. Checks: preserve original PR-to-line mapping and quantities; prevent double allocation; separate incompatible specifications. Output: consolidated demand batch with traceability to all origin PRs.
03 — Procurement verification
Actions: validate specifications, quantity, stock, open orders, supplier feasibility, intended procurement route and requirement date. Decision: Validated or Returned with reason. Control: reviewer should not silently alter approved technical specifications.
04 — RFQ preparation
Actions: define line items, required specs, bid currency/unit, target quantity, delivery location, quality documents, sampling requirement, bid cutoff and quotation conditions. Output: RFQ-YYYY-NNNNN , version V1 , status Ready to Publish . Control: change after publish issues an RFQ revision and notifies relevant suppliers.
05 — Supplier shortlist
Actions: choose active and eligible manufacturers/traders, vendor-country calendar and verified email contacts; document single-source situations. Checks: tax/business registration where required, sanction/compliance checks under company policy, previous performance and supplier status. Output: invited supplier list with invitation eligibility and rationale.
06 — Per-supplier secure URL generation
Actions: create a random, high-entropy, supplier-specific token bound to RFQ/version/vendor; store a hash server-side; provide individual access to only that vendor's RFQ. Controls: HTTPS, token expiry, rate limits, access logs, revocation, no competitor visibility, and optional one-time email verification/OTP. Never include sensitive prices in URL parameters. Output: expiring submission link per recipient.
07 — Automated invitation
Actions: send branded invitation with subject, RFQ details, due time in supplier timezone, instructions and secure link. Checks: supplier email validation, queued/sent/failed status, retry with idempotency (avoid duplicate invitations). Output: transmission log and copy of email.
08 — Supplier RFQ submission
Actions: supplier confirms company/contact information, enters line-wise offer and technical details, uploads documents and samples schedule, saves draft, then signs declaration and submits. Checks: required fields, numeric ranges and units, allowed document types, antivirus scanning, supplier-country/timezone, version and RFQ deadline. Output: immutable submitted quotation revision, QUO-YYYY-NNNNN . Drafts are not evaluated.
09 — Deadline control
Actions: check server receipt timestamp against calculated deadline; send reminder; disable final submission at cutoff unless extension is approved. Output: Submitted on Time , Not Submitted / Expired , or Extended . Control: authoritative server time, not browser/device clock; extension reason, approver and notifications are recorded.
10 — Comparative statement
Actions: compare supplier quotes line by line, normalize currency and UOM using dated controlled FX assumptions, compare Incoterms on a defined landed-cost basis, show technical compliance and blanks. Control: lowest unit price is not automatically the lowest total landed cost; disclose estimations separately from supplier-confirmed figures. Output: versioned CS, supplier comparison and shortlist for testing/negotiation.
11 — Sample collection and testing
Actions: request and track samples, courier/AWB, receipt, lab test plan, controlled result uploads, appearance, fusing/bond strength and other item-specific tests. Output: sample/test ID, test result and supporting report. Exception: approved sample waiver or alternate evidence must have its own authorization.
12 — Technical acceptance
Actions: QA/Marketing determines Pass , Conditional Pass , Fail or Waived by Approval against documented tolerance/specification. Control: conditional pass cannot be treated as a full pass without an approved deviation. Output: technical clearance for negotiation/award; rejected offers remain archived.
13 — Negotiation
Actions: track negotiation rounds for price, discounts, MOQ, payment, shipment, packing, Incoterms and quality undertakings; request formal revised quotation where material. Controls: immutable original quotation, revision numbers, maker/checker rights and audit log. Output: agreed commercial terms, without discarding old quote versions.
14 — Supplier recommendation
Actions: Procurement prepares final CS, technical summary, total estimated landed cost, risk notes, negotiation savings and recommended vendor with written rationale. Output: supplier award recommendation package for sequential acknowledgment/approval.
15 — Marketing ACK
Review: fit to requested materials, operational need, quantities, approved sample/QA outcome and delivery suitability. Action: Acknowledge or Return for Clarification ; acknowledgment is not the final financial approval.
16 — Finance approval
Review: budget availability, cash requirements, FX exposure, duties/freight assumptions, bank charges, payment terms, advance/LC exposure, landed cost and supporting justification. Action: Approve , Return or Reject ; where approval thresholds apply, system enforces them. Output: timestamped decision and finance comments.
17 — ED / final approval
Review: end-to-end award case, supplier recommendation, prior approvals, deviations and material exposures. Action: Final Approve , Return or Reject ; delegation must be documented. Control: final approval locks supplier, quantity, rates, currency, Incoterms, payment and shipment commitments for PO production.
18 — Automated PO generation
Actions: generate PO-YYYY-NNNNN directly from approved fields, attach terms and technical annexures, create versioned PDF and archive a checksum/snapshot. Validation: vendor email; ship-to/bill-to; UOM/qty; price/currency; tolerance; payment; delivery; Incoterms named location; signatures or approval reference. Output: Ready to Dispatch ; validation failures go to a correction queue rather than sending an incomplete PO.
19 — Automated PO distribution
Actions: email PDF and secure acknowledgment URL to selected supplier after validation; log send result; retry technical failures without duplicate PO numbers. Output: PO Sent with notification ID/time or PO Delivery Failed alert to Procurement.
20 — Supplier acknowledgment
Actions: supplier opens link, accepts PO or flags a specific discrepancy. Controls: supplier cannot modify PO; dispute creates a change-request task, with possible reapproval. Output: Accepted , Disputed , or Awaiting Acknowledgment with reminder and escalation rules.
21 — Proforma Invoice validation
Actions: collect PI from supplier and compare seller/buyer, material/specs, HS code where available, quantity, unit price, total, currency, Incoterms/place, shipping schedule, payment and bank details. Controls: vendor bank details change requires independent verification; differences create documented exceptions; no silent acceptance of mismatched commercial terms. Output: PI Matched / Approved for Import Processing or correction request.
22 — Import, shipment and GRN
Actions: apply applicable bank/LC/TT process, track import authorization and documents, expected/actual shipment, packing list, commercial invoice, transport documents, customs clearance, arrival, quantity received, QA inspection and warehouse posting. Exceptions: partial shipment, late dispatch, short/over receipt, damage, nonconforming goods, insurance claim, supplier debit/credit note. Output: GRN and receiving discrepancy records, updated stock and financial liabilities where appropriate.
23 — Invoice match, payment and closure
Actions: match PO, GRN, accepted quantity and invoice; check advances, taxes/duties, deductions and debit notes, obtain payment authorization; process and reconcile bank payment. Controls: three-way match and segregation of duties; prevent duplicate invoice/payment; release only approved/eligible amounts. Output: payment reference, reconciled supplier balance and closed PO when open commitments are resolved.

6. China RFQ deadline engine and secure link expiry
Figure 2. RFQ publication, supplier response and expiry/extension decision
Default business policy proposal: standard RFQ = 3 China working days ; urgent RFQ = 2 China working days , with authorized reason; counting begins from the next China working day after invitation/publish ; close at 17:00 China Standard Time (Asia/Shanghai) on the last counted working day. These values should be configuration, not hard-coded policy.
Confirm supplier's country and assigned work calendar ( CN by default for China-based supplier).
Calendar includes Chinese public holidays and officially designated weekend make-up workdays ; yearly source data are maintained/verified by an administrator before use.
Convert all local display times to/from a UTC database timestamp; China CST here means China Standard Time , not US Central Standard Time.
Calculate closing_date = Nth eligible working date after RFQ issue date under the proposed counting convention; apply configured closing hour.
Display exact deadline in China time and OIL/Bangladesh time, with timezone name and UTC offset.
Send reminder, proposed one China working day before close to non-submitters.
At cutoff, revoke final-submit ability; preserve already submitted records; drafts remain historical but unsubmitted.
Authorized extension requires revised due date, explanation and approver; notify supplier of the change and record original deadline.
If country differs from China, use corresponding approved country calendar; do not assume a global Monday–Friday calendar.
If holiday/calendar feed is unavailable, block uncertain deadline publication and alert Procurement instead of silently computing a possibly wrong cutoff.
Example (illustrative, not a live computed statutory-calendar deadline): If a China supplier is invited on a Monday and Tuesday–Thursday are confirmed working days, a three-working-day response closes Thursday at 17:00 China time (15:00 Bangladesh time). An official holiday or make-up working day may change this calculation.
Link security and submission states
Invitation queued → Sent → Viewed → Draft started → Submitted OR Not submitted → Expired OR Deadline extended → Submitted . Token theft mitigation should include token hashing, short-lived or vendor-bound validation, HTTPS, rate limits, optional email OTP, and sensitive action logging. The supplier must never view other suppliers' bids.

Suggested decision logic (policy subject to approval)
Gate 1: Eligibility — active/compliant supplier, quote received by valid deadline.
Gate 2: Technical qualification — tested pass or formally approved deviation/waiver.
Gate 3: Commercial comparison — landed cost, payment exposure and on-time supply.
Gate 4: Procurement recommendation — preferred supplier with rationale and alternative.
Gate 5: Sequential approval — Marketing ACK → Finance APP → ED/Final APP.
A sample optional weighted score is Cost 35%, Technical &amp; Quality 30%, Delivery 20%, Commercial Terms 10%, Supplier History 5% ; these percentages are illustrative only , must be approved before deployment and cannot override mandatory QA rejection gates.
Negotiated savings: where meaningful, show (baseline comparable landed cost − final comparable landed cost) separately from budget savings. Keep exchange-rate effects and quantity changes visible rather than incorrectly attributing them to negotiation.

9. Purchase Order, supplier acknowledgment and PI rules
Figure 5. Automated PO to import and payment closure
PO content checklist
PO number/date; buyer legal name Osman Interlinings Limited (OIL) and address; supplier legal details/contact; delivery and invoice addresses; originating PR/RFQ/quote IDs; material code/description/technical annex; quantity/UOM/tolerance; unit and extended amount; currency/tax assumptions; Incoterms 2020 named place; shipment schedule and loading/discharge port; packing/marking; sample/quality acceptance standard; payment and banking terms; claims/replacement provisions; required trade documents; approved internal reference; supplier acknowledgment instructions.
Automatic dispatch conditions
Final approval = valid AND PO source version = final approved version AND required fields complete AND supplier email verified AND no unresolved blocking deviations → create approved PDF → enqueue email → log message ID → track send/delivery error. Never regenerate a different legal PO under the same identifier without a revision.
PO acknowledgment and PI
Supplier selects Accept or Dispute on secure URL. The system records time and identity. PI must match buyer/seller identities, description/specification, quantities, price, totals, currency, shipping window, Incoterms place, payment terms and beneficiary bank details. Discrepancies create a change request; changes to approved commercial fundamentals route back for authorized review.
10. Import, receiving and payment lifecycle
Import sub-process: validated PI → import documentation/banking checks → LC/TT/advance approval per policy → shipment booking/confirmation → production milestone tracking → supplier dispatch document set → transit/ETA tracking → customs clearance and duty/tax documentation → delivery to OIL → GRN and QA receiving report → supplier invoice matching → payment/advance adjustment → order closure.
Potential documents: PI, commercial invoice, packing list, transport document (BL/AWB as appropriate), certificate of origin when required, insurance evidence, LC/TT documents, import permission/customs documentation, test certificate/COA, GRN and supplier credit/debit note. Exact mandatory document rules depend on import method and applicable regulations; Finance/Import must validate them.
Exception handling: partial shipment, over/short receipt, wrong specification, failed QA, late delivery, damaged goods, missing shipping papers, demurrage, exchange-rate change, invoice mismatch and duplicate invoicing each create an exception case with assignee, root cause, corrective action and closure approval.
Three-way match: PO ↔ GRN/accepted quantity ↔ Supplier invoice. Material deviations block or partially restrict payment until resolution. Paid amount, advance balances, deductions and remaining PO commitments should reconcile.

15. Dashboards, reports and management KPIs
Operational dashboard: open PRs, upcoming deadlines, quotations pending, suppliers not responding, tests pending, negotiation queue, cases at each approval level, PO email delivery failures, PIs unmatched, late shipments, open GRNs and due vendor invoices.
Management dashboard: total procurement value, approved vs pending orders, estimated landed cost by material/supplier, time from PR to RFQ, RFQ response rate, RFQ-to-PO cycle time, negotiated cost improvements, sample first-pass rate, supplier on-time delivery, rejection/claim rate, unacknowledged POs, import delays, payment aging and exception closure time.
Suggested formulas and definitions:
KPI
Formula / definition
RFQ response rate
Quotations validly submitted ÷ suppliers invited × 100
PR-to-PO lead time
PO dispatch timestamp − initial approved PR submission timestamp
On-time delivery
Deliveries by contractual date ÷ total deliveries due × 100
Sample acceptance rate
Samples accepted ÷ samples fully evaluated × 100
Quote-to-PO conversion
Awarded RFQs ÷ RFQs closed and evaluated × 100
PO acknowledgment SLA
Supplier ACK timestamp − PO email sent timestamp
Three-way-match exception rate
Invoices with PO/GRN/invoice mismatch ÷ invoices checked × 100
Negotiated savings
Like-for-like comparable baseline cost − approved comparable final cost
Baseline historical data and target thresholds are not supplied ; reporting must not present invented savings or performance percentages.
16. Non-functional, security and integration requirements
Security: role-based permissions, least privilege, HTTPS, encryption at rest as appropriate, MFA for internal approvers, supplier URL token protection, file scanning, session protections, per-action audit log, document integrity snapshots and backup/recovery procedures.
Operational integrity: event-driven email queue, delivery/retry log, idempotent automatic actions, clear error queues, system health monitoring and approval/task SLA alerts.
Usability: desktop-first internal portal and mobile-responsive supplier forms; English UI and supplier-ready wording; consistent identifiers; searchable document library; accessible error messages.
Timekeeping: UTC timestamp storage with Asia/Shanghai and Asia/Dhaka presentation; verified country-level working-day calendars and DST support for other supplier countries.
Master data: supplier, material/specification, UOM conversion, FX rate source/date, port, terms, approval authorities, China holiday calendar, notification templates and mandatory technical fields.
Potential integrations: inventory/ERP, accounting/AP, warehouse/GRN, email provider, document storage, bank/import register and BI dashboards. Integration targets and APIs are TBD .
Record retention: OIL-defined legal/document retention period, access controls, archiving and deletion schedule; subject to applicable regulatory requirements.
Data quality: mandatory code lists, duplicate checks, units, versioning, cross-checks and controlled import of supplier master data.
